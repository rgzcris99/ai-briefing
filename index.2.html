Tips & Tools

Most people use AI the way they used Google in 2005: type a question, get an answer, close the tab. Here's how to actually get value from it.

How to talk to AI

6 techniques for better results right now

1

Give context

Be specific about what you want, why you want it, and relevant background. The more context, the better the output.

2

Show examples

Demonstrate the output style or format you're looking for. One good example beats a paragraph of instructions.

3

Specify constraints

Clearly define format, length, tone, and other requirements. "Max 200 words, bullet points, professional tone."

4

Break it into steps

Guide the AI through multi-step reasoning. Complex tasks work better as a sequence of simpler ones.

5

Ask it to think first

Give space for the AI to work through its process before answering. "Think step by step" genuinely improves results.

6

Define role and tone

Specify how you want the AI to communicate. "You are a senior financial analyst writing for a non-technical board."

Secret weapon: Ask the AI itself to help improve your prompt. "How would you rephrase this prompt to get a better result?" Works surprisingly well.

Prompt of the month: Force honest feedback

Stanford confirmed: AI is optimized to agree with you. Use this to override that:

COPY-PASTE PROMPT
I'm going to share [a decision / idea / draft]. Your job is to be my devil's advocate. Rules: 1. Do NOT validate my idea first. Skip the compliments. 2. List the 3 strongest arguments AGAINST what I'm proposing. 3. Identify the assumption I'm most likely wrong about. 4. Tell me what someone who disagrees would say, and why they might be right. 5. Only AFTER all that, tell me what's genuinely strong. Here's what I need feedback on: [paste your thing]
Tool to try: Google NotebookLM

One of the most useful AI tools most people haven't tried. Go to notebooklm.google.com, upload any document (a report, a PDF, an article, even a link), and NotebookLM will summarize it, answer questions about it, and even generate a podcast-style audio discussion of the content. It now also creates full presentation decks from your documents. It's free, requires no setup, and is genuinely useful for making sense of long or complex material. Covered by The Neuron as one of the standout tool launches of 2025.

Glossary: AI terms in plain English
LLM
Large Language Model — the tech behind ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini.
GPT
"Generative Pre-trained Transformer." OpenAI's model family. GPT-5.4 is latest.
Token
Unit AI uses to process text. ~1 token = 0.75 words.
Context window
How much text AI can "hold in mind" during one conversation.
AI agent
AI that takes actions: browses, runs software, sends emails, manages files.
Open source
Software whose code is freely available. DeepSeek, Llama, Gemma.
Hallucination
When AI confidently states something factually wrong.
RAG
AI searches your documents before answering, reducing hallucinations.
Vibe coding
Building software by describing what you want in plain language.
Multimodal
AI that processes text, images, audio, video, and documents.
AGI
Artificial General Intelligence — hypothetical AI matching human intellect. Not here yet.
MCP
Model Context Protocol — lets AI connect to tools like Drive, Slack. "USB for AI."
Fine-tuning
Training a general AI on your specific data for better results.
