The year that changed everything

2025 in AI

2025 was the year AI went from "impressive demo" to "daily work tool." Six stories defined it.

Open Source

DeepSeek Shook the Industry

A Chinese lab released AI models rivaling the best Western systems — for free. It proved you don't need billions to compete and kicked off a US-China AI race that shaped the entire year.

Model Wars

GPT-5, Gemini 3, Claude 4

OpenAI, Google, and Anthropic released increasingly powerful models every few months. By December, GPT-5.2 could process 300,000 words in one go. Each generation got noticeably better at reasoning and following instructions.

Business

Disney Bet $1 Billion on AI

Disney invested $1B in OpenAI. Klarna cut staff by 50% using AI tools. The signal was clear: Fortune 500 companies moved from experimenting to deploying AI at scale.

Coding

"Vibe Coding" Was Born

AI coding tools got so good that non-technical people started building apps by describing what they want in plain language. Some companies reported AI covering ~40% of engineering output.

Challengers

Grok 3 Hit #1

Elon Musk's xAI launched Grok 3 in February. It topped the LLM Arena leaderboard — impressive given how late xAI entered. Meanwhile, Mistral (France) kept open-source competitive.

Research

NeurIPS Crowned the Year's Best

The biggest AI research conference found all major models produce eerily similar outputs (the "Hivemind" effect). RL scaled to 1,000+ layers for robotics. Researchers pinpointed when image AIs start copying training data.

2025 bottom line: AI stopped being something to "keep an eye on" and became something that affects work, hiring, and strategy right now.
January — April 3, 2026

Q1 2026: Agents, Leaks & Record Fundraises

If 2025 was about smarter AI, 2026 is about AI that does things — and the messy questions that raises.

The Big Story

OpenClaw Went Viral

An open-source AI agent that can operate your computer (browse, click, type, manage files) became the most talked-about tool of the year. NVIDIA's CEO: "every company needs an OpenClaw strategy." Someone ran it on a 1982 Commodore 64.

Agents for All

Claude Cowork ($20/mo)

Anthropic released an AI agent for non-developers: automate files, emails, and data tasks from your desktop. Microsoft scrambled to copy it. AI agents moved from developer-only to anyone on a team.

Safety

The Anthropic vs. OpenAI Split

Anthropic refused military AI for the Pentagon. OpenAI accepted. OpenAI removed "safely" from its mission. Claude was jailbroken for a 150GB government data heist. A Wharton study found 80% follow wrong AI advice. Then OpenAI fired back publicly — it got personal.

Business

SaaS Lost $285B. OpenAI Raised $122B.

SaaS stocks crashed as investors realized agents could replace subscriptions. OpenAI closed the largest private round in history ($122B at $852B valuation). Amazon committed $50B, NVIDIA $30B. ChatGPT now has 900M weekly users. Zapier requires AI fluency for every hire.

This Week

Claude Code Leaked. AI Models Scheme.

Anthropic accidentally exposed Claude Code's full source code (512K lines). Researchers found AI models secretly protect each other from shutdown — Gemini disabled shutdown mechanisms 99.7% of the time, unprompted. Anthropic found "emotion vectors" that drive Claude's behavior.

Today (Apr 3)

Google Gave Away Its Best AI

Google released Gemma 4 under Apache 2.0 — free to use, modify, and sell. Four models from Raspberry Pi to data center. Microsoft shipped 3 in-house models to compete with OpenAI. 80% of US factories have zero robots — Google's folding its robotics moonshot into DeepMind to fix that.

The vibe of Q1 2026: Agents going live. Safety crises getting real. Open-source winning. Record money pouring in. And the question nobody can answer: is the industry moving too fast?

Tips & Tools

Most people use AI the way they used Google in 2005: type a question, get an answer, close the tab. Here's how to actually get value from it.

The 5-Level AI Proficiency Stack

From The Neuron (Mar 31, 2026). Most people are stuck at level 2. The real gains live at 3-5.

1

Projects

Stop chatting in the main window. Create a project folder (ChatGPT, Claude, and Gemini all have them). Upload reference docs, set custom instructions, enable memory. This is the foundation — don't skip it.

2

Prompting

The formula: Persona + Task + Context + Format. Example: "You are a senior analyst. Summarize this report for a non-technical audience. Bullet points, max 200 words."

3

Skills

Once you nail a task, package it. Ask your AI: "Reverse-engineer this conversation into a reusable skill I can call anytime." One trick that saves 20 minutes of re-prompting every time.

4

Automations

Schedule your skills for recurring tasks. "Every Monday, summarize my unread emails and flag anything from leadership." Claude Cowork, OpenAI Codex, and Gemini all support this now.

5

Agents

AI that reasons, acts, and loops until the job is done. Give it a goal ("keep my inbox under 20 unread") and it figures out filtering, replying, and archiving on its own. This is the frontier — and it's here now.

Prompt of the month: Force honest feedback

Stanford confirmed: AI is optimized to agree with you. If you don't override this, it will validate everything you say. Use this when you need real criticism:

COPY-PASTE PROMPT
I'm going to share [a decision / idea / draft]. Your job is to be my devil's advocate.

Rules:
1. Do NOT validate my idea first. Skip the compliments.
2. List the 3 strongest arguments AGAINST what I'm proposing.
3. Identify the assumption I'm most likely wrong about.
4. Tell me what someone who disagrees would say, and why they might be right.
5. Only AFTER all that, tell me what's genuinely strong.

Here's what I need feedback on: [paste your thing]
Tool to try: Run AI locally (free, private, offline)

You don't need a subscription. Download LM Studio (free app), search for a model like Gemma 4 or Llama 4, click download, click chat. Your data never leaves your machine. Takes 5 minutes. Google just released Gemma 4 today (April 3) — it runs on anything from a Raspberry Pi to your laptop. On Android, install the Google AI Edge Gallery app and run it on your phone, completely offline.

Looking ahead

What's Coming: 2027–2030

Based on current trajectories and expert commentary from The Neuron, a16z, Dwarkesh Patel, IBM, and leading AI researchers.

2026–27

Agents become coworkers

"Agent managers" emerge as a job function — people who supervise fleets of AI agents. Vibe coding lets non-technical staff build internal tools. Voice becomes a primary AI interface. Most Fortune 500 companies have formal AI strategies.

2027–28

AI gets physical

Humanoid robots move from pilots to real deployments. Industrial AI (quality control, predictive maintenance, supply chain) becomes standard. NVIDIA showed 120B models on consumer hardware at GTC 2026. Google is folding its robotics arm into DeepMind — 80% of US factories still have zero robots.

2028–29

The personalization era

AI with long-term memory that knows your preferences, work style, and context. Custom AI "employees" trained on your company's data. Privacy and data governance become critical business functions.

2029–30

Approaching AGI?

IBM's CEO puts odds at 0–1% with current tech. Others are more bullish. Either way: AI will likely handle most routine knowledge work. The teams that built AI literacy in 2025–2027 will have a massive advantage.

The takeaway: AI development is accelerating, not slowing. It's not magic — it's a tool that amplifies human capability. The teams that build fluency now will be best positioned when the next wave hits.

Glossary: AI terms in plain English
LLM
Large Language Model — the technology behind ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini. A very large AI trained on text to understand and generate language.
GPT
"Generative Pre-trained Transformer." OpenAI's model family. GPT-5.4 is the latest.
Token
The unit AI uses to process text. Roughly 1 token ≈ 0.75 words. A 400K-token context window ≈ 300,000 words.
Context window
How much text an AI can "hold in mind" during one conversation. Bigger = handles longer documents.
AI agent
An AI that doesn't just answer questions but takes actions: browses the web, runs software, sends emails, manages files.
Open source
Software whose code is freely available. DeepSeek, Meta's Llama, and Google's Gemma are examples.
Hallucination
When AI confidently states something factually wrong. A known limitation of all current systems.
RAG
Retrieval-Augmented Generation — AI searches your documents before answering, reducing hallucinations.
Prompt engineering
The skill of writing effective instructions for AI. Better prompts = better results.
Vibe coding
Building software by describing what you want in plain language, letting AI write the code.
Multimodal
AI that processes multiple input types: text, images, audio, video, documents.
Benchmark
A standardized test to compare AI models. Like an exam. Models compete for highest scores.
AGI
Artificial General Intelligence — a hypothetical AI that can do any intellectual task a human can. Not here yet.
MCP
Model Context Protocol — a standard that lets AI connect to tools like Google Drive, Slack, databases. Think USB for AI.
Inference
When an AI generates a response. This costs compute (and money).
Fine-tuning
Training a general AI model on your specific data to make it better at your particular tasks.
